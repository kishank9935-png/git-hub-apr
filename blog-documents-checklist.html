Important Documents to Check Before Buying Land in Uttar Pradesh

By APR GROUP OF COMPANY • Published: Real Estate Insights • 7 Min Read

Table of Contents

  • 3. Khasra & Shajra Map (Cadastral Survey Map)

Purchasing land is a legal milestone that transfers property rights permanently to the buyer. In Uttar Pradesh, land documentation follows a well-defined revenue framework. Understanding the essential papers guarantees complete peace of mind and legally sound ownership.

1. Original Title Deed (Bainama / Sale Deed)

The registered sale deed (bainama) confirms how the current seller acquired ownership of the property. Check:

  • The seller's identity matches the name recorded in previous registry deeds.
  • The unbroken chain of ownership (minimum 12 to 30 years historical trail where available).
  • That the deed was officially registered with the local Sub-Registrar Office (Tehsil office).

2. Khatauni (Record of Rights) Verification

In Uttar Pradesh, the Khatauni is the primary revenue record showing land ownership under the UP Revenue Code (accessible online via the official UP Bhulekh portal). It verifies:

  • The registered co-sharers or sole owners of the Khata.
  • The land classification and total area in hectares/acres.
  • Whether any bank mortgage, government stay, or revenue attachment is registered on the land parcel.

3. Khasra & Shajra Map (Cadastral Survey Map)

While Khatauni establishes ownership, the Khasra and village village map (Shajra) identify the exact geographical parcel number on the revenue grid. This confirms the physical location of the land within the village boundary.

4. Non-Encumbrance Verification (Bar-Mukti)

An encumbrance search confirms that the land has not been pledged as loan collateral to a financial institution and is free from civil litigation or court dispute.

5. Mutation (Dakhil Kharij) Protocol

Executing a registered sale deed transfers legal title, but entering the buyer's name in revenue records requires Mutation (Dakhil Kharij). Always ensure that the seller has completed mutation in their name before you execute your purchase.

6. Clear Boundary Identification in the Registry

When the registry deed is drafted, the four boundaries (Chauhaddi) must be clearly recorded: What lies to the East, West, North, and South (such as front sector road, adjoining plot numbers, or open boundaries). This eliminates future boundary disputes.
